01

Threshold

Housing

A house is not a list of rooms. First the threshold: where the door turns, the height the window sits at, the tread the hand meets. The plan is the result of these three.

Hall and rooms are as large as they need to be. Light enters from one face and stays on that face in the evening. The red line on the plan marks the threshold. The work starts there.

02

Attic

Housing

An attic often stays a store room. First the trusses and rafters are measured; what carries and what was added later is separated.

Light comes in through the roof. The window sits between two rafters; the timber is not boxed in, it stays visible.

03

Window

Interior

Inside, every junction shows. Joint, sill and door frame are chosen from the same family. Colour is used to separate one surface, not to paint every wall.

Furniture is drawn as a tool with a known height. In section the table sits at sitting height. The red frame marks where the light comes in.

04

Wet room

Interior

In a small bathroom every centimetre is a decision. No material is chosen before the drain, the fall and the pipes have their place in the drawing.

Terrazzo floor, lime-plastered walls, a stone basin. Few materials, visible junctions.

05

Facade

Existing

An old building is recorded first. A crooked wall is not a fault to be corrected. The new work sits against it. What is removed is written down. What is added does not have to imitate the old.

It answers the old building’s height, rhythm and weight. On the elevation the red dashed line is the proposed opening. It does not break the existing windows; it has to explain why it is there.

06

Bay window

Existing

A timber bay window is the most fragile part of a facade. It is surveyed first; the rotten parts are separated from the sound ones.

The repair keeps the old dimensions. A new piece does not imitate the old one; it continues the same section.

07

Courtyard

Civic

The metre between street and building is often left empty. A canopy, a threshold, a seat, the shade of a tree. A neighbourhood is read from these pieces.

The scale is small: the hour at which a court is in shade. In plan the black mass is the existing building. The red dashed line is the canopy added to it.

08

Stepped street

Civic

On a slope the stair is the street. Riser height and landing intervals are set by the walker’s breath.

A tree, a place to sit, a light for the evening. All three are drawn on the same sheet, at the same scale.
